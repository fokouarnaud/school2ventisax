Modern refrigeration oils are highly refined products, free of unwanted substances such as sulphur compounds and waxes. They must have the lowest possible moisture content and be handled so as to avoid absorbing moisture or air: close the can immediately after use.

The main functions of refrigeration oil are:

- to lubricate moving parts, so delaying wear (bearings, liners, pistons, rings, valves...);
- to help cool the compressor;
- to improve the sealing of the compression parts.

There are two main families: **mineral oils** (naphthenic, paraffinic) and **synthetic oils**: alkylbenzene (AB), polyalphaolefin (PAO), PAO + AB, polyolester (POE), polyalkylene glycol (PAG).

## Qualities of a good refrigeration oil

| Quality | Definition |
|---|---|
| Stability | Resistance to breakdown, dielectric strength and behaviour with the various components |
| Viscosity | Resistance to flow, depending on temperature and on how much refrigerant dissolves in the oil |
| Solubility | Ability of the oil to dissolve refrigerant vapour |
| Kinematic viscosity | Resistance to flow compared with water, in mm²/s (water = 1 mm²/s) |
| Hygroscopy | Ability of the oil to absorb moisture from the air; POE and PAG oils are highly hygroscopic |
| Miscibility | Ability of the oil to mix with liquid refrigerant into a uniform mixture |
