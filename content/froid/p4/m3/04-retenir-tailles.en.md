## The outside diameter

Refrigeration copper is named by its **outside diameter** (OD, <span lang="fr">diamètre extérieur</span>): the width of the tube measured **over the wall** with a caliper, from one outer edge to the other. A 1/2 tube is 12.7 mm OD; its inside diameter is smaller by two wall thicknesses.

## Two numbers, then halve

Remember **1 inch = 25.4 mm**, then halve, again and again:

| Fraction | Value | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25.4 mm | the inch |
| 1/2 | 12.7 mm | the half |
| 1/4 | 6.35 mm | the quarter, the liquid line of a split |
| 1/8 | 3.2 mm | the eighth |
| 1/16 | 1.6 mm | the sixteenth |

Each refrigeration tube is the sum of two pieces of this ladder, easy to add in your head:

| Tube | Working | About |
|---|---|---|
| 3/16 | 1/8 + 1/16 = 3.2 + 1.6 | 4.8 mm |
| 1/4 | the quarter | 6.35 mm |
| 5/16 | 1/4 + 1/16 = 6.35 + 1.6 | 7.9 mm |
| 3/8 | 1/4 + 1/8 = 6.35 + 3.2 | 9.5 mm |
| 1/2 | the half | 12.7 mm |
| 5/8 | 1/2 + 1/8 = 12.7 + 3.2 | 15.9 mm |
| 3/4 | 1/2 + 1/4 = 12.7 + 6.35 | 19.05 mm |
| 7/8 | 1 - 1/8 = 25.4 - 3.2 | 22.2 mm |
| 1 1/8 | 1 + 1/8 = 25.4 + 3.2 | 28.6 mm |

**The other way round**, from a reading in mm to the size: start from the nearest anchor (6.35 or 12.7), then add or take away an eighth (3.2) or a sixteenth (1.6). 9.5 mm is 6.35 + 3.2: 1/4 + 1/8 = 3/8. 7.9 mm is 6.35 + 1.6: 5/16.

## Checking in sixteenths

To check, you can also count everything in **sixteenths**: 1/4 = 4/16, 3/8 = 6/16, 1/2 = 8/16, 5/8 = 10/16, 3/4 = 12/16, 7/8 = 14/16. One sixteenth being 25.4 ÷ 16 = 1.5875 mm, about 1.6 mm, the number of sixteenths × 1.6 gives the diameter to within a few tenths: 5/16 ≈ 5 × 1.6 = 8 mm.

| Size | In sixteenths | Quick estimate (n × 1.6) | Exact diameter (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3/16 | 3/16 | 4.8 | 4.7625 |
| 1/4 | 4/16 | 6.4 | 6.35 |
| 5/16 | 5/16 | 8 | 7.9375 |
| 3/8 | 6/16 | 9.6 | 9.525 |
| 1/2 | 8/16 | 12.8 | 12.7 |
| 5/8 | 10/16 | 16 | 15.875 |
| 3/4 | 12/16 | 19.2 | 19.05 |
| 7/8 | 14/16 | 22.4 | 22.225 |
| 1 1/8 | 18/16 | 28.8 | 28.575 |

Up to 3/8, each size is one sixteenth (1.6 mm) bigger than the one before; above it, the step becomes two sixteenths (1/8 inch, 3.2 mm).

## One size, one use

| Tube | Common use |
|---|---|
| 3/16 | Small lines in domestic refrigeration (fridge, freezer) |
| 1/4 | Liquid line of a split system |
| 5/16 | Suction line of domestic appliances and small units |
| 3/8 | Suction line of a small split (about 2.5 to 3.5 kW) |
| 1/2 | Suction line of a split of about 5 kW |
| 5/8 | Suction line of a split of about 7 to 10 kW |
| 3/4 | Suction line of large splits and cold room units |
| 7/8 and 1 1/8 | Headers and suction lines of commercial systems and refrigeration packs |

These uses are guides: the diameters of a system are always read in the manufacturer's instructions.

## The other pipes of the installation

| Pipe | How it is named | Common sizes | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper, metric series | Inside × outside diameter, in mm | 4 × 6 to 34 × 36 | Water, gas, refrigeration |
| Threaded steel | Thread size in BSP inches: not the pipe diameter | 1/2" BSP: 21.3 mm OD; 3/4": 26.9 mm | Large systems, chilled water |
| PVC drain pipe | Outside diameter, in mm | 16 and 20 (condensate of one split), 32 (condensate of several units), 40 and 50 (appliances), 63 (room header), 100 (stack) | Condensate and drains |
| Flexible electrical conduit (<span lang="fr">gaine ICTA</span>) | Outside diameter, in mm | 16 (interconnecting and control cable), 20 (supply of one unit), 25 (large section) | Cable routing |
| Multilayer pipe | Outside diameter, in mm | 16, 20, 26 | Water only, never refrigerant |

## Do not confuse

- **3/8 and 10 mm**, **5/8 and 16 mm**: close but different diameters. A 3/8 fitting does not take a 10 mm tube.
- **1/2 copper and 1/2" BSP**: the 1/2 copper tube is 12.7 mm OD, the 1/2" BSP steel pipe 21.3 mm.
- **Conduit 16 and PVC 16**: same diameter, but one protects cables and the other drains water.

## Tools carry the tube size

The flaring bar hole, the flare nut, the bender former and the swaging tool head are marked with the tube size: choose the tool by the tube size (1/4, 3/8...), never by eye.
