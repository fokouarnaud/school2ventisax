## General points

Brazing joins parts, of the same material or not, with a molten filler metal. During brazing the parts do not melt, only the filler does. Copper melts at 1085 °C.

| Type | Melting point of the filler |
|---|---|
| Soft soldering | Below 450 °C |
| Hard brazing | Above 450 °C |

## Conditions for a good brazed joint

1. Clean the joint surfaces to be abraded, with polishing cloth or special brushes.
2. Deburr the cut to round the edges.
3. For parts of the same diameter, swage (<span lang="fr">emboutir</span>) one of the two tubes, according to the direction of flow.
4. Coat the parts to be joined with the flux that suits the alloy, in just enough quantity.
5. Place the parts in their final position.
6. Adjust the flame and heat the part.
7. As soon as the flux melts, touch the tip of the filler rod to the joint. The molten filler is drawn by capillary action into the gap between the parts: the flame heats the joint, not the rod.
8. Remove flux residue by washing with water, preferably hot, to avoid cooling too quickly.

Never heat a line containing refrigerant under pressure: there is a risk of explosion and of decomposition of the refrigerant.
