**Welding** joins two metals by melting and fusing the parts in contact. There is torch fusion welding, arc fusion welding and electric resistance welding.

**Brazing** joins metal parts, without melting them or applying pressure, with a filler metal made liquid by a torch flame. The parts are joined when the filler solidifies. This technique can also join different metals.

| Type | Melting point of the filler |
|---|---|
| Soft soldering | Below 450 °C |
| Braze welding | Between brazing and welding |
| Hard brazing | Above 450 °C |

**The filler** (<span lang="fr">brasure</span>) is the metal used for brazing. It is generally an alloy with a low melting point. Refrigeration uses silver brazing alloy, copper-phosphorus alloy and soft solder (tin-silver, tin-lead, etc.).

**Fluxes** form a continuous layer on the joint surface during brazing, to prevent oxidation.

**Scale** (<span lang="fr">calamine</span>) is an oxide layer produced on metals at high temperatures. It results from oxidation of the metal by the oxygen in the pipe during brazing.
