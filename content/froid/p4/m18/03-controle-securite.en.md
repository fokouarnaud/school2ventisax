## Inspecting the weld

| Finding | Bead appearance |
|---|---|
| Normal bead | Even bead, clean and regular, good penetration |
| Current too low | Poor penetration, high bead reinforcement, slag inclusions, risk of lack of fusion |
| Current too high | Deep penetration, undercut, spatter, collapsing weld pool |
| Travel too fast | Poor penetration, narrow irregular bead with long ripples |
| Travel too slow | Deep penetration, collapsing weld pool |
| Arc too long | Wide flat bead, irregular, fine spatter |

## Safety

- **Arc radiation**: cover all parts of the body and wear a helmet with a filter shade suited to the current; screen people nearby.
- **Burns**: thick, non-synthetic gloves and clothing; handle parts with tongs.
- **Fumes** (oxides, nitrous fumes): ventilation or extraction.
- **Electrical risk**: the open-circuit voltage of the set can be dangerous, especially in wet or metal surroundings; work in the dry, with dry gloves, never change an electrode bare-handed, keep cables in good condition, and switch the set off before any work on it.
- **Fire**: move combustibles away, keep an extinguisher to hand and watch the area after the work.
- **Magnetic fields**: place the earth clamp as close as possible to the work.

## Cleaning the bead

Once the bead is finished, remove the slag (<span lang="fr">laitier</span>) that formed on the surface to protect the weld pool, with a chipping hammer, then wire-brush the bead to clear the last pieces. Wear safety glasses while chipping.
