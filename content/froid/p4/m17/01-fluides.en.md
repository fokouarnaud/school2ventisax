Once built, a refrigeration system needs refrigerant to produce cooling. Two precise, effective methods are used to put it in: the mass of refrigerant and the suction superheat.

Refrigerants can be classified in several ways, for example by their environmental impact.

## Pure inorganic substances

Water (H2O), coded R718; ammonia (NH3), R717; carbon dioxide (CO2), R744. These natural refrigerants have been used since the early 20th century. They do not affect the ozone layer; CO2 has a global warming potential of 1, very low, and ammonia has no greenhouse effect but is toxic. They have useful thermodynamic properties.

## Hydrocarbons

The most used are butane (R600), isobutane (R600a), propane (R290), cyclopropane (RC270) and propylene (R1270). They are flammable (class A3): they are used where the charge is small, in a ventilated room with no ignition source.

## Halogenated hydrocarbons

| Family | Composition | Examples | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFC (chlorofluorocarbons) | Chlorine, fluorine, carbon | R11, R12 | Banned: they destroy the ozone layer |
| HCFC (hydrochlorofluorocarbons) | Chlorine, fluorine, carbon and hydrogen | R22, R123, R141b | Transitional, being phased out |
| HFC (hydrofluorocarbons) | Fluorine, carbon, hydrogen, no chlorine | R134a, R404A, R410A | Most used in recent systems; some have a high greenhouse effect |

Follow local regulations (<span lang="fr">réglementation locale</span>) on permitted refrigerants.
