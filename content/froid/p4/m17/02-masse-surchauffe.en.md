## Charging by mass

This is the most reliable method, recommended by manufacturers. Read the refrigerant quantity on the rating plate, then, using electronic scales, put the exact mass stated by the manufacturer into the system.

## Charging by superheat

This is a way to check the charge when the mass to put in is no longer known. Charge while checking the superheat, so as to end up with the right quantity of refrigerant.

**Superheat** (<span lang="fr">surchauffe</span>) = temperature at the evaporator outlet - evaporating temperature (read on the BP gauge, with the refrigerant's table). It is expressed in kelvin (K).

**Example**: evaporating temperature -10 °C, measured temperature at the evaporator outlet -4 °C: superheat = -4 - (-10) = 6 K.

A superheat of 4 to 8 K is common, but the right value depends on the system: follow the manufacturer's figure. Too much superheat usually means a shortage of refrigerant; too little, an overcharge, with a risk of liquid returning to the compressor. For a zeotropic blend, the evaporating temperature is read from the dew value of the table. On a system with a thermostatic expansion valve, the charge is better checked by subcooling, as the valve sets the superheat itself.
