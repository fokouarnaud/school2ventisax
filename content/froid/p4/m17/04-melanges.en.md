A refrigerant blend behaves in one of two ways:

| Blend | Behaviour | Examples | Charging |
|---|---|---|---|
| Azeotrope (R500 series) | Behaves like a single refrigerant: it boils and condenses at constant temperature for a given pressure | R502, R507A | Liquid or vapour |
| Zeotrope (R400 series) | Its components boil at different temperatures: temperature changes during evaporation and condensation (temperature glide) | R404A, R407C, R410A | Always as liquid |

A zeotrope charged as vapour does not keep its composition: the most volatile component leaves the cylinder first, and the refrigerant put in no longer has the intended properties. It is therefore always charged as liquid, preferably into the liquid line, or in small amounts on the BP side with the compressor running, through a charging restrictor.

After a large leak of a high-glide zeotrope, the remaining composition has changed: common practice is to recover the refrigerant and recharge completely.

R410A has a very small glide (<span lang="fr">glissement</span>): it is charged as liquid but behaves almost like an azeotrope.
