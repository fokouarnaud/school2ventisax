## The five criteria for choosing

The choice of a fitting depends on the circuit you are working on and on the aim. In practice, ask yourself five questions, in this order.

| Criterion | Question to ask | What the answer decides |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pipe material | What are the pipes to be joined made of? | Copper, steel, PVC or electrical conduit: each material has its own fittings; a copper fitting is never solvent-welded onto PVC |
| 2. Diameter | Do the pipes have the same outside diameter? | Same diameter: equal coupling, elbow or tee; different diameters: reducer or reducing tee |
| 3. Function | What must the fitting do? Must it stay removable? | Extend (coupling), change diameter (reducer), change direction (elbow), create a branch (tee) |
| 4. Joining method | How is it assembled? | Brazing for a permanent joint, flare fitting for a removable joint, flameless press fitting where a flame is forbidden, solvent welding for PVC, push fit for conduit |
| 5. Fluid and pressure | Which fluid flows, at what pressure? | Refrigerant under pressure: brazed copper, flare or a press fitting designed for refrigeration; condensate: PVC; multilayer pipe: water only, never refrigerant |

## The range of fittings

| Fitting | Function | Common variants |
|---|---|---|
| Coupling, or socket (<span lang="fr">manchon</span>) | Extend a pipe of the same diameter | Brazed, press, solvent-welded; threaded brass: male/male (union, <span lang="fr">raccord union</span>), male/female, female/female |
| Reducer (<span lang="fr">réduction</span>) | Change from one diameter to another | Brazed (for example 1/2 × 1/4); threaded steel: male/female reducer |
| Elbow (<span lang="fr">coude</span>) | Change direction | 45°, 90° and 180° (return bend); threaded brass: 90° and 135°; other angles are made by bending (module 4) |
| Tee (<span lang="fr">té</span>) | Create a branch | Equal or reducing (branch smaller than the run); flare male/male/male or male/female/male |

A reducer only joins neighbouring diameters: to go from 1/4 to 1 1/8, use several reducers in a row, or rethink the route.

## Male or female

A **male** (<span lang="fr">mâle</span>) fitting carries its thread on the outside; a **female** (<span lang="fr">femelle</span>) fitting, on the inside. A male always goes with a female.

- On a flare fitting, the flare nut (<span lang="fr">écrou dudgeon</span>, female) presses the flared pipe onto the threaded cone of the fitting (male).
- Two flared pipes are therefore joined by a male/male union; two male ports, by a female/female coupling.
- Brazed, press and solvent-welded fittings have female ends (sockets) that receive the pipe.

## Workshop examples

| Situation | Suitable fitting |
|---|---|
| Extend a brazed 1/4 liquid line | 1/4 brazed coupling |
| Join a 1/2 pipe to a 1/4 pipe | 1/2 × 1/4 brazed reducer |
| Join two 3/8 flared pipes that must be removable | 3/8 male/male union |
| Go round a beam on a 5/8 suction line, in a room where a flame is forbidden | Two 5/8 press elbows at 45° |
| Connect the drain of a split (PVC 20) to a PVC 32 header | PVC 32 × 20 tee, solvent-welded |
| Extend the supply conduit of a unit | Conduit coupling 20, push fit |
