The main functions of refrigeration oil in a system are:

- to lubricate moving parts, so delaying wear (bearings, liners, pistons, rings, valves...);
- to help cool the compressor;
- to improve the sealing of the compression parts.

The two main families are **mineral oils** (naphthenic, paraffinic) and **synthetic oils**: dialkylbenzene, naphthenic-benzene (semi-synthetic), polyalphaolefin (PAO), PAO + alkylbenzene (AB), polyolester (POE), polyglycol, polyalkylene glycol (PAG).

| Quality | Definition |
|---|---|
| Stability | Resistance to breakdown, dielectric strength, behaviour with the various components |
| Viscosity | Resistance to flow, depending on temperature and refrigerant solubility |
| Solubility | Ability to dissolve refrigerant vapour |
| Kinematic viscosity | Resistance to flow compared with water, in mm²/s (water = 1 mm²/s) |
| Hygroscopy | Ability to absorb moisture from the air |
| Miscibility | Ability to mix with liquid refrigerant into a uniform mixture |

Oil that has absorbed moisture or broken down becomes acidic: acid attacks the winding varnish and the metals. Hence the tests in this module (<span lang="fr">tests d'acidité</span>).
