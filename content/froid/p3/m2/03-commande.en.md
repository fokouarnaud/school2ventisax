## The contactor

A contactor (contacteur) is a control device that can make or break a more or less high current in a power circuit. It has a **coil** whose job is to open or close several control contacts and several power contacts.

| Marks | Part |
|---|---|
| A1-A2 | Coil (electromagnet) |
| 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 | Power contacts (main poles) |
| 13-14 | Normally open auxiliary contact (NO) |
| 21-22 | Normally closed auxiliary contact (NC), when fitted |

A contactor is marked with the letters **KM** followed by its number (KM1, KM2…).

### Technology

Seen exploded, a contactor has:

- the electromagnet: a fixed part (magnetic circuit and coil) and a moving part;
- a shading ring, which stops the moving part from chattering on AC;
- a return spring, which opens the contacts when the coil is no longer supplied;
- the fixed and moving contacts;
- a clip-on system for adding auxiliary contact blocks.

When the coil is energised, the moving part is pulled in and closes the power contacts; when it is de-energised, the return spring opens them.

## Coil-and-contact relays

A coil-and-contact relay is an **electromechanical** relay: a switch controlled by a low-power DC or AC voltage. Its switching part drives mains loads, up to 10 or 16 A depending on the model. There are 4 types of relay depending on their contacts:

| Type | Meaning | Contacts |
|---|---|---|
| SPST | Single Pole Single Throw | 1 simple contact (open or closed) |
| SPDT | Single Pole Double Throw | 1 changeover contact |
| DPST | Double Pole Single Throw | 2 simple contacts switched together |
| DPDT | Double Pole Double Throw | 2 changeover contacts switched together |

## Solid-state relays

These components do the same job as a coil-and-contact relay, but without any mechanical part. Switching is done by a **triac** driven by an **optocoupler**, which keeps the control side electrically isolated from the power side.

### Uses of electronic relays or micro-relays

- Electric heating
- Light fittings
- Any application where motors are switched: garage doors, motorised beds, household appliances, washing machines, and so on.
