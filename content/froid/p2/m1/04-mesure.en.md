## The electrician's measuring instruments

| Instrument | Quantity measured | Unit | Connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammeter | Current flowing through a piece of equipment | ampere (A) | In series in the circuit |
| Voltmeter | Voltage between two points | volt (V) | In parallel across the equipment terminals |
| Ohmmeter | Resistance, for example of a coil | ohm (Ω) | Across the component, **with the circuit isolated (dead)** |
| Capacitance meter (<span lang="fr">capacimètre</span>) | Capacitance of a capacitor | farad (F), in practice microfarad (µF) | Capacitor disconnected and discharged |
| Multimeter | Combines several functions: voltage, current, resistance, continuity, sometimes capacitance | depends on the function | Depends on the function selected |
| Clamp meter | Current, without breaking the circuit | ampere (A) | The jaws surround a single conductor |
| Non-contact voltage tester | Presence of the line conductor | none | Held near the conductor, it signals the line with a sound or a light |

## Rules of use

- Before every measurement with a multimeter, select the function and range that match the quantity measured. If unsure, start with the highest range.
- Connect the black probe to the COM terminal and the red probe to the terminal for the function (V/Ω or A).
- Ohmmeters and capacitance meters are always used on a **dead** component: the instrument supplies its own test current. Connecting an ohmmeter to a live circuit gives a wrong reading and can destroy the instrument.
- A capacitor can stay charged after the supply is switched off: discharge it before touching or measuring it.
