Electrical equipment falls into three main categories: **protective devices**, **control** (switching) devices and **loads** (current-using equipment).

## A. Protective devices

- **Circuit breakers** protect an installation against overloads, short circuits and overheating. A **thermal-magnetic** circuit breaker (MCB) trips on overload (thermal part) and on short circuit (magnetic part). A **residual current device** (RCD, <span lang="fr">différentiel</span>) detects leakage (residual) currents and protects people.
- A **thermal overload relay** protects electric motors against overloads (prolonged overcurrents).
- A **fuse switch disconnector** (<span lang="fr">sectionneur porte-fusible</span>) isolates all or part of an installation during work; its fuses protect against short circuits.

## B. Control devices

- **Switches** control equipment such as lamps or switched sockets from one point. There are one-way single-pole or double-pole switches, two-way switches, double switches, ventilation switches or rotary switches (to choose the speed of the extract ventilation), and dimmer switches, which switch the light on and off and vary its brightness.
- **Push buttons** can be normally open (NO) or normally closed (NC).
- A **contactor** is an electromechanical device able to make, carry and break currents under normal conditions, including operating overloads. Its coil, supplied by the control circuit, closes its power contacts.
- **Timer blocks**, fitted to a contactor, delay switching a piece of equipment on or off. There are **on-delay** timer blocks (delayed closing) and **off-delay** timer blocks (delayed opening).

## C. Capacitors

The course concentrates on capacitors, which are common in refrigeration work. A **capacitor** is a component designed to store an electric charge. In a single-phase induction motor, it creates the phase shift that lets the motor start and run. Its capacitance is expressed in farads, in practice in microfarads (µF). There are:

- **start capacitors**, used only while the motor starts;
- **run capacitors**, used as long as the motor runs.

Their rating plate shows the capacitance (µF), the tolerance (±5% for example) and the maximum voltage (450 V for example): a replacement capacitor must have the same capacitance and at least the same voltage rating.
