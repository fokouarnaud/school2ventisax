## Target competence

Learners will be able to define accurately the basic concepts of electricity, such as electric current, voltage and current intensity. They will also be able to use measuring instruments correctly to determine the electrical quantities in an installation.

## Basic concepts

1. **Electric current**: the ordered movement of electrons in a conductor.
2. **Current** (<span lang="fr">intensité du courant</span>): the quantity of electricity (electric charge) passing through a cross-section of the conductor in one second. It is expressed in **amperes (A)**.
3. **Resistance**: how much a conductor opposes the flow of electric current. Its unit is the **ohm (Ω)**.
4. **Ohm's law**: the voltage, or potential difference, across a resistance equals its resistance multiplied by the current flowing through it: **U = R × I**, with U in volts (V), R in ohms (Ω) and I in amperes (A).
5. **Power**: the work done in one second: **P = W / t**, with P in watts (W), W in joules (J) and t in seconds (s). In direct current, or for a resistive load: **P = U × I = R × I² = U² / R**.
6. **Alternating current (AC)**: a current that reverses direction periodically. It is characterised by its voltage in volts and its frequency in hertz (50 Hz or 60 Hz depending on the country). Its voltage and current vary sinusoidally. It can be distributed as single-phase or three-phase.
   - Advantages: more efficient transmission over long distances; the most widely used supply for electrical and household appliances.
7. **Direct current (DC)**: a current that flows in one direction only. It is found in portable electronic devices (phones, tablets, laptops), battery electric vehicles, uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), medical equipment such as defibrillators and pacemakers, network communication systems and household appliances.

## Example

A lamp supplied at 230 V draws 0.26 A. Its power is P = U × I = 230 × 0.26 ≈ 60 W, and its hot resistance R = U / I = 230 / 0.26 ≈ 885 Ω.
