## Protection

**Collective protection** is equipment that does not require the worker to put anything on: permanent or temporary guardrails, scissor lifts, scaffold towers.

**Personal protection** is equipment the worker must put on correctly before working at height: for example a safety harness attached to a suitable anchor point with an energy-absorbing lanyard.

## Things to do

- Do as much of the work as possible from the ground.
- Use scaffolding rather than ladders.
- Make sure workers can get to and from the work area at height safely.
- Make sure the equipment is suitable, stable, strong enough for the task, and regularly maintained and inspected.
- Take precautions when working on or near fragile surfaces.
- Provide protection against falling objects.
- Plan emergency evacuation and rescue procedures.

## Things not to do

- Overload ladders: assess the equipment and materials workers will carry, and check the maximum load on the ladder's pictogram or label.
- Overreach on a ladder or stepladder and lose your balance.
- Rest a ladder against fragile surfaces (glazing, plastic gutters).
- Use ladders or stepladders for strenuous, long or heavy tasks: keep them for light, short jobs (30 minutes at a time at most, for example).
- Let someone who is not competent (without the necessary skills, knowledge and experience) work at height.
