## Tips for working at height

### 1. Use guardrails

Whenever possible, use guardrails: passive protection is the simplest way to keep workers safe and comply with regulations. There are guardrail systems for almost every type of roof: free-standing for flat or low-pitched roofs, parapet-mounted, for steel deck roofing, etc. Prefabricated guardrails can be fitted temporarily or permanently. Once in place, they are the easiest fall-protection system to use and set up.

### 2. Choose the right PPE

Harnesses that meet the standards do not all have the same quality, performance and ergonomics, or the same price. Sometimes the difference is the brand; more often it is the features (extra D-rings, flame-resistant material…). A more expensive harness may simply be more comfortable.

Study your needs and the likely risks. For workers welding at height, a standard nylon harness is not suitable: Kevlar may be a better option. Think about your employees' comfort, which makes cooperation easier. Harnesses are not one size fits all: everyone must be able to adjust theirs.

### 3. Inspect your PPE

Equipment that is not inspected can fail at any moment. Harnesses and lanyards must be inspected regularly by a competent person (who can recognise the danger and has the authority to fix it), and by the user before each use. Users must know what to look for, what is acceptable or not, and what to do if they find a problem. A quick check before work can save a life.

### 4. Record inspections and understand fall distance

Inspection results are recorded in a register available for checking.

Fall-arrest equipment is useless if you hit the ground before it engages. A worker 3 or 4 m off the ground with a 2 m energy-absorbing lanyard is not protected: you must add 1 to 2 m for the absorber to deploy, the length of the body below the D-ring and the give in the harness and anchorage. Allow at least 6 to 8 m of height before using a 2 m lanyard with an energy absorber.

### 5. Choose a proper anchor point

PVC pipe, decorative steel… many supports are **not** proper anchor points. An anchor point must withstand a force of **10 kN for 3 minutes** (according to the standard). A fixing on structural steel with a suitable clamp, a lift or a manufactured anchorage system can be suitable if properly installed.

### 6. Choose the best option: scaffolding, platform or ladder

Sometimes scaffolding with guardrails is the best solution. On some platforms you need a harness and lanyard properly attached. With a ladder, fall-protection requirements become more complex. Assess the situation carefully and choose the equipment best suited to the task and the place.

### 7. Use ladders correctly

Ladders cause many domestic and industrial accidents because using them seems obvious. Check that a ladder is the best way to do the task and that employees know how to use it (4:1 angle ratio, three points of contact). Train them to use the tools safely.

### 8. Know the rules for moving on a roof

Rules for working at height are among the most demanding and least understood: many workers think certain protection methods are allowed for them when in fact they are not.

### 9. Use aerial lifts correctly

Anyone in an aerial lift, at any time and at any height, must be properly attached: to the anchor point provided on the lift, without wrapping the lanyard around the rails, with a lanyard actually secured at the working height. In a scissor lift, being attached is not always required, but if you forget to close the gate or chain, the rails no longer protect you.

### 10. Train and practise regularly

To work safely at height, employees must be properly trained. Training is required by law, and the risk of error is too great for someone without the necessary knowledge.
