## Basic electrical safety rules

### 1. Do not underestimate the risks

Static electricity poses no direct risk to people, but the electricity in our homes and workplaces does. Any work on or near an electrical installation or piece of equipment is risky.

### 2. No makeshift jobs

Use a socket outlet to make connections. Never push bare wires into a socket.

### 3. Use equipment correctly

Electrical equipment is designed to protect its user, but it can wear out over time or after misuse.

- Before each use, check that your tools are in good condition (casing, cable and plug).
- Never leave a portable tool on its own and plugged in, even for a short absence.
- Never leave an extension lead live with nothing plugged into it.
- Always throw away makeshift hand lamps and any equipment that cannot be repaired.

### 4. Do not mix up cables

An electrical cable contains several conductors identified by different colours. These "wires" have different functions and are not interchangeable. Getting tangled in the wires is always a risk, for you or for the equipment. A cable must only be used to supply a device: using it for anything else is dangerous.

- Unplug a device by pulling the plug, never the cable.
- Never use cables to move or lift a device.
- Avoid walking or driving over cables: their insulation can be damaged.

### 5. Beware of water

Water and electricity do not mix: water greatly increases the risk of electric shock, especially with power tools in poor condition.

- Never put out a fire on an electrical installation with water, unless the water is sprayed as a mist or the installation is switched off.
- Before cleaning, check that the electrical equipment is designed to be cleaned with water.
- When it rains, put equipment under cover.

### 6. Respect access authorisations

A worker who opens an electrical cabinet or resets a protective device must be authorised to do so by their employer; otherwise they must not do it. Never work in an electrical cabinet without the proper clearance.

### 7. Do not touch what you do not know

This precautionary principle always applies: what looks switched off can be powered again, and what is stopped can start up again.

- Do not go near a fallen overhead line unless you are authorised.
- Do not touch an uninsulated or bare cable unless you are sure it is dead.

### 8. Locate and use the emergency stop

The emergency stop cuts the power when people or equipment are in danger. In an emergency, cut the power immediately, and make sure emergency stops remain easy to reach.

### 9. Keep safe distances

Keeping away from bare live parts prevents accidental contact, directly or through a conductive object (pole, bar, metal ladder…). On building sites, this distance is **3 m** for voltages up to 50 kV and **5 m** above 50 kV.

- Keep your distance near overhead lines: they are rarely insulated.
- Do not store scrap metal or reinforcing bars under overhead lines.
- Keep hand-held tools away from bare live parts.
