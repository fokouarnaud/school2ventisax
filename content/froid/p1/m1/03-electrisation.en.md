## Safety around live equipment

When part of the body is exposed to a voltage, an electric current flows through it and can cause various kinds of harm.

An **electric shock** is the result of current flowing through the body. Its effects vary:

- **minor shock**: the shock is felt but has no after-effects;
- **serious shock**: the shock can cause breathing, muscle or heart problems;
- **fatal shock**: when it causes death, it is called **electrocution**.

**Burns** are caused by current flowing through the body (internal burns) or by an electric arc (external burns). They are usually serious injuries that can be fatal. This harm can come with other effects, such as a fall from height.

## Danger thresholds

The effects of current depend mainly on its **intensity** (in amperes, A, or milliamperes, mA) and on how **long** it flows through the body:

- from **0.5 mA**, involuntary muscle contractions can occur;
- a **10 mA** current flowing through the body for a few seconds is enough to cause surface burns and circulation or breathing problems that may be irreversible;
- a **40 mA** current, the current flowing through a 10 W lamp, can kill a person.

> **Beware of low voltage!** "Low voltage" is no guarantee of safety: low voltage goes up to 1,000 V AC. Any voltage above 50 V is potentially fatal. Sockets in homes and offices are supplied at 230 V.
