## Sheet 6.1: identifying a gas cylinder

The NF EN 1089-3 standard sets the colour of a cylinder's shoulder (the top). General rule: **one hazard = one colour**. The code depends on the gas's physical, chemical or toxicological properties. Four main hazards are defined:

| Shoulder colour | Hazard |
|---|---|
| Yellow | Toxic and/or corrosive |
| Red | Flammable / combustible |
| Light blue | Oxidising |
| Bright green | Inert / neutral |

If a gas has several hazards, the shoulder takes the colour of the main one.

Some gases have their own colour: nitrous oxide (dark blue), oxygen (white), acetylene (maroon), carbon dioxide (grey), hydrogen (dark red), helium (brown), nitrogen (black), argon (dark green).

> **Note**: when NF EN 1089-3 came in, cylinders whose colour changed had to carry two opposite "N" letters on the shoulder. They have since been repainted at inspection and the letter N is no longer needed.

## Sheet 6.2: transporting gas cylinders

**General points**

- Close the valves, even if the cylinders are empty.
- Never let cylinders drop hard onto the ground.
- Cylinders must always have their protective cap (tulip) screwed on.
- Never transport cylinders with their regulating accessories (pressure regulator, torch…): remove them after closing the cylinders.
- No smoking, avoid any spark.
- Wear PPE: goggles, gloves, safety shoes.

**Moving by hand**

- Never drag or lift a large cylinder by its cap.
- Only roll a cylinder on its base over a flat surface and a short distance; otherwise, use a cylinder trolley.

**Transport by vehicle**

Carriage of dangerous goods by road is governed by the ADR (European Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road), which applies to all industrial gases. Its obligations can be relaxed depending on the type of gas and the quantities carried.

Gases fall into three transport categories:

- **category 1**: toxic gases, except anhydrous ammonia (UN1005) and chlorine (UN1017), limited to 50 kg in total per transport unit;
- **category 2**: flammable gases (F);
- **category 3**: asphyxiant (A) and/or oxidising (O) gases.

Maximum quantity per transport unit to stay below the regulatory thresholds: category 1: 20; category 2: 333; category 3: 1,000 (net mass in kg for liquefied or dissolved gases, nominal capacity in litres for compressed gases). Examples: propane, butane or acetylene: 333 kg; hydrogen: 333 litres; argon, nitrogen, oxygen, helium: 1,000 litres. If several categories are carried together, the weighted sum of the quantities must not exceed 1,000.

The ADR considers an empty, uncleaned package to be as dangerous as a full one.

> **Important**: always prefer transport by a professional gas supplier or a specialist carrier, especially for toxic gases.

**Vehicle fit-out**: proper securing of the cylinders; open, covered or permanently ventilated vehicle; airtight separation between the driver and the cylinders; powder fire extinguisher of at least 2 kg; explosion-proof lamp. Staff must be trained to ADR requirements.

**Recommendations**

- Check the cylinders before loading and secure them: even lying in a boot, a cylinder becomes a dangerous projectile in a crash or rollover.
- Store cylinders upright, keep empty ones apart from full ones and oxygen apart from flammable gases, balance the load.
- Do not leave cylinders in a vehicle unnecessarily and protect them from strong sunlight.
- If there is a leak: pull over, air the vehicle, close the valves and tell the supplier.
- If there is ignition or fire: do not approach, keep onlookers away and call the emergency services.

## Sheet 6.3: precautions for use

**Storage room**

- Ideally outdoors, for permanent natural ventilation; otherwise, mechanical ventilation.
- Secure, easy access, floor in perfect condition, temperature suited to the gases stored (a canopy protects from sun and weather).
- Mark off the area according to the risk and display the emergency instructions.
- For flammable gases, follow the explosive atmospheres regulations (ATEX).
- If needed, ventilated storage cabinets for toxic and corrosive gases.
- Limit the quantities stored; for hydrogen, prefer a hydrogen generator where possible.
- Separate cylinders by hazard family; do not store unused cylinders in workshops.

**Handling**

- Use a suitable cylinder trolley.
- Secure cylinders to a fixed point, at 2/3 of their height, with a chain.
- Do not ride in a goods lift or lift with the cylinders.
- Wear suitable PPE (safety shoes, handling gloves…).
- Never move a cylinder without its protective cap (tulip or bell).
- Never try to catch a falling cylinder.
- Lift a cylinder with bent legs and a straight back.

**Commissioning and use**

- Prefer gas distribution from central supply points outside buildings, and keep rooms with cylinders permanently ventilated.
- Match quantities to needs and keep an up-to-date inventory of cylinders with their requalification dates.
- Secure hoses with a clamp suited to the pressure; mark piping with the standard colours.
- Use suitable fittings, valves and regulators; never grease a valve; open valves slowly, by hand and without forcing.
- Never tinker with a faulty valve or remove a cap not designed to be removed.
- Secure hoses so they cannot whip (anti-whip cables or chaining to the wall); do not join hoses together; keep them as short as possible.
- Check hoses, fittings, threads and seals regularly; respect their replacement dates; tolerate no leaks.
- Install gas detectors suited to the gases used, linked to an audible and visual alarm and, if needed, to the gas shut-off and forced ventilation.
- Avoid any ignition source near a flammable gas; never decant one cylinder into another.
- Store empty cylinders like full ones, always upright.
- Return to the supplier any damaged cylinder or one past its requalification date (10 years).
- Get the technical data sheets for the gases, mark off the areas of use, display the emergency instructions.

> **Note**: the next requalification date is shown on a plastic ring fitted around the valve.
