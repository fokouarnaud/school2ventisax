## Introduction

About two million people are killed by their work every year: this is a worldwide figure taken from the latest ILO estimates. At the same time, the number of workplace accidents worldwide, fatal or not, is estimated at 270 million a year.

To reduce this figure and get closer to zero risk, workers must master the risks of their job and adopt the right safety measures, because:

- work that kills is not inevitable;
- accidents do not just happen;
- illnesses do not strike at random.

So you must be able to tell apart dangerous situations, near misses, minor accidents and workplace accidents, and take every necessary measure to guard against them.

## Dangerous situations, near misses and accidents

**The problem**: when you are around danger all the time, you get used to it and stop noticing it. You can walk past a dangerous situation a thousand times, and then one day… So you must:

- wake up your senses;
- become attentive and alert again;
- be an **active player** in your own safety.

**Stop needless dangers and risks: take action.**

A **near miss** is a situation where the accident did not happen, but very nearly did. The consequences could have been serious (thermal or chemical burns, electric shock, a fall from height, etc.). A **minor accident** is one that did happen, but with light injuries that only needed first aid.

These situations must be reported and all their causes identified: the immediate causes (the obvious ones) as well as the root causes (structural or organisational).

The report must be as **complete** and **precise** as possible. It states:

- a description of the place;
- the circumstances;
- the facts;
- anything that helps understand what happened.

A **workplace accident** is a sudden event, external to the victim, that caused an injury and happened during and because of the work.

> **Every workplace accident must be reported immediately to your line management.**
