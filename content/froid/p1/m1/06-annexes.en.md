## Appendix 1: blast effect

Blast means the injuries caused by the shock wave of an explosion. It also throws people and debris and has a thermal effect (burns).

**Main possible injuries**

- **Ears**: damage to the eardrum, hearing loss or even deafness, bleeding.
- **Digestive system**: swelling of air-filled organs (stomach, intestines), internal bleeding, abdominal pain; signs can appear several hours after the explosion.
- **Airways**: nosebleeds, severe breathing difficulties, cyanosis (bluish skin).
- **Skin and mucous membranes**: burns of varying severity.
- **Flying objects**: fractures, wounds, torn-off limbs.

**What to do**

Because the severity of the injuries is not obvious, while waiting for help:

- lay the victim down (unless they prefer to sit up because of breathing difficulties);
- assess their condition and start CPR if needed;
- deal with urgent injuries (press on a bleeding wound, treat burns…);
- reassure them, watch them and keep them covered until medical staff take over.

A 24 to 48-hour hospital stay for observation is usually needed.

> **Medical advice required**: never let anyone leave the scene of an explosion, even if they seem unhurt, before a doctor has examined them.

## Appendix 2: oxygen level in the air and effects on people

| Oxygen level | Effects |
|---|---|
| 22 % | Normal breathing |
| 19 % | Fatigue, bloating, impaired judgement |
| 16 % | Feeling unwell, rapid pulse |
| 14 % | Shortness of breath, dizziness |
| 10 % | Nausea, vomiting, fainting |
| 6 % | Respiratory arrest, cardiac arrest |
| 0 % | Death after 3 breaths |

## Appendix 3: hazard pictograms

| Pictogram | Hazards |
|---|---|
| Flame over a circle | Oxidising gases, liquids and solids |
| Flame | Flammable gases, aerosols, liquids and solids, self-reactive, pyrophoric or self-heating substances, substances that release flammable gases in contact with water, organic peroxides |
| Skull and crossbones | Acute toxicity (oral, dermal or by inhalation) |
| Gas cylinder | Compressed, liquefied, refrigerated liquefied or dissolved gases |
| Corrosion | Corrosive to metals, skin corrosion, serious eye damage |
| Yellow triangle "asphyxiation" | Risk of asphyxiation |
| Yellow triangle "snowflake" | Cryogenic fluid present |

## Appendix 4: identifying pressurised cylinders

Every cylinder carries a label that gives:

1. the name of the gas;
2. its nature and chemical composition;
3. the hazard pictogram(s);
4. the UN code for transport;
5. the signal words and hazard statements (H…);
6. the precautionary statements (P…);
7. the supplier's name and contact details;
8. other information (pressure, quantity of gas).

Examples: labels on an argon cylinder and a hydrogen cylinder.
