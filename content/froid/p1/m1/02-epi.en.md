## Personal protective equipment (PPE)

PPE means any device or means designed to be worn or held by a person to protect them against one or more risks that could threaten their health or safety. PPE must meet the safety requirements of international regulations, which set out:

- protection suited to each type of risk;
- the highest possible level of protection;
- comfort, ergonomics and quality of the equipment;
- a yearly check of the PPE by a competent person.

Companies' obligations to use PPE are also set by local and international directives. PPE must be tested regularly by qualified people. **At the slightest doubt, the equipment must be withdrawn and replaced immediately.**

## Head and face protection

- **The safety helmet**: mandatory whenever there is a risk of injury from falling objects or knocks to the head.
- **The helmet with built-in face shield**: mandatory whenever there is a risk of flying particles or substances, or radiation that could injure the eyes or face. It is mandatory for electrical work in the low-voltage work zone, within 30 cm of bare live parts.

## Insulation

- **Insulating gloves** protect against electric shock when working live or near live parts. There are two types: latex gloves, worn with silicone-treated leather over-gloves for mechanical protection, and composite gloves, which are mechanically stronger. They are chosen by class, which matches the working voltage.
- **Insulating shoes and boots** insulate the person from the ground so that current cannot flow through them. They carry the marking of a category 3 PPE (electrical risk in the European Union).
- **Insulating mats** insulate the operator from the ground. They are matched to the installation's voltage, identified by class, and must not be used on wet floors.
- **Insulating stools**, mainly used for high voltage, are checked visually before each use and once a year by a qualified person.

## Arc flash protection

An electric arc results from a short circuit. Almost instantly, it produces effects that are often dramatic:

- a thermal effect, the main feature of the arc;
- a blast effect caused by the very fast release of energy in a small volume of air (similar to an explosion);
- a light effect with strong ultraviolet and infrared radiation;
- an ionisation effect that can trigger further arcs on nearby live parts;
- a detonation that can cause deafness.

Arc flash workwear protects you in the event of an electric arc.
