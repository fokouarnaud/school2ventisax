## Structure of an internship report

**1. The cover** must include:

- the intern's surname and first names;
- the title and type of report;
- the name and position of the professional supervisor;
- the name and position of the academic supervisor;
- the name and logo of the company and of the training centre;
- the name of the course or qualification being prepared;
- the year of study;
- the date or period of the internship.

It should be attractive without being cluttered and is never numbered. It can be followed by a flyleaf, then a title page, or directly by the table of contents.

**2. The flyleaf** (optional): a blank page.

**3. The dedication page** (optional).

**4. The table of contents or report outline**: one to two pages, at the start of the document, before the acknowledgements and introduction. By reading the contents, a few pages and the conclusion, the reader should understand how the report unfolds: the approach, the direction, the hypotheses and how the central question is resolved. Choose your headings carefully and precisely. Parts, sections and paragraphs follow a logical order, clearly numbered and paginated.

**5. Acknowledgements**: after the contents and before the introduction. First to the company placement supervisors and the academic supervisor (welcome, supervision, knowledge, methods and advice), then to the company staff, family and friends for their support.

**6. The list of abbreviations** (optional): after the acknowledgements and contents, it explains the abbreviations and acronyms.

**7. The introduction**: one to two pages briefly outlining the report. It sets out the reasons for choosing the job, course, company or sector, then the company's general and economic context, and finally the definition of the subject (why this subject, why it matters, its setting).

**8. The body**, the most important part, written with perspective rather than as a day-by-day account.

First, present the company and its environment: overview, general organisation (organisation chart), history, business sectors, markets, customers and suppliers, competition, key figures, outlook (strengths, weaknesses, threats, opportunities), technological change, management's goals.

Then present your assignment: the position held and its place in the organisation chart, key events, objectives and stakes, methods and resources used, the people you worked with, responsibilities held, results achieved, difficulties met and solutions found.

**9. The conclusion**: usually one page, presenting the report's main conclusions, then its limits and possible extensions, with suggestions and recommendations.

**10. The bibliography**: after the conclusion, detailed references (author, title, year of publication, edition, journal…) of the reading and works used or cited.

**11. The appendices** complete and support your argument: useful but non-essential documents (diagrams, press articles, book extracts, charts, sales documents, statistics, market studies, photographs…). Each appendix is referred to in the body of the report.
