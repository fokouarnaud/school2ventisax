## What is an internship report?

The internship report sums up the experience you gained during your work placement. It is written for the examiner, but it also helps you take stock of your future career. This demanding but compulsory exercise is instructive: it helps you review the skills and knowledge you have gained. Its conclusions then help you plan your career (which job or sector to aim for, for example).

## Why write an internship report?

The examiner wants to understand how useful your internship was and what it brought to your studies. You must show that the internship helped you:

- gain in-depth knowledge of a business sector;
- get an overall view of the company, its structure and its activities;
- deepen your knowledge of a trade or discover new ones;
- absorb practical knowledge and processes;
- master and understand what was at stake in the tasks you were given;
- face and solve blockages and difficulties;
- develop skills (rigour, team spirit, creativity, project management…).

The report must answer the following questions:

- Why did you choose this internship?
- Did you enjoy it?
- Did it meet your expectations and the objectives set?
- Were you valued as an intern? For what reasons?
- Were your personality and results noticed?
- Were your teamwork and people skills useful to the department?
- What effect does the internship have on your career plan? Will you change the direction of your job search or continue in the same sector?

Also show how the theories and results studied during your training helped you during the internship. Illustrate your report with a theoretical diagram studied in class: that way, your training will have been useful to the company.

## How to write it

**Practical advice**: be critical. Not just a light description of the company, but a thorough analysis of the company and its tasks. Intelligently critique the work processes, the organisation chart, the jobs that exist or are missing, and suggest new solutions. Highlight the tasks you carried out. Analyse the company, its strengths and its weaknesses.

**Form**

- Short, precise, information-rich sentences.
- Flawless spelling and grammar.
- A number of pages that depends on the length of the internship and the scale of the tasks.
- Charts to make reading easier, without weighing the report down with irrelevant figures or documents.
- Take notes during the internship: once you have left the company, some information will be hard to get.
