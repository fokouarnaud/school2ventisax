## What are the dangers?

Working in a confined space is dangerous because of harmful chemical fumes, lack of oxygen and the risk of fire. There are other dangers too: flooding, drowning, and asphyxiation caused by dust, grain or other contaminants.

## What should the employer do?

Avoid any work in confined spaces whenever possible. Otherwise, assess the risks of the space in question and plan how to control them. For example:

- if harmful fumes are present, ventilate the space or remove the fumes;
- if there is a risk of exposure to a liquid or gas, check that the supply valves can be closed, and check the weather forecast for work inside storm drains;
- if there is not enough oxygen to breathe, provide breathing apparatus or ventilate the space to raise the oxygen level and remove pollutants;
- make emergency arrangements: know whether people are all right and not overcome by fumes, and be able to get them out if they lose consciousness (you cannot simply rely on the emergency services).

## Things to do

- Be aware of the risks inside a confined space.
- Make sure the person doing the work is competent and trained, both for the job and for using emergency equipment.

## Things not to do

- Work in a confined space unless it is essential.
- Ignore the risks: a space declared safe one day is not necessarily safe the next.
- Let anyone enter a confined space without being sure it is safe.
