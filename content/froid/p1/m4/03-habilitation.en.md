## Electrical clearance

Refrigeration technicians work as electricians on various pieces of electrical equipment and must be informed of electrical risks. Electrical clearance (habilitation électrique) recognises that a person is able to carry out this type of work safely. There are several clearance levels depending on the voltage range and the staff.

| Voltage range (1st letter) | Staff qualification (index) | Type of operation (2nd letter) |
|---|---|---|
| **B**: low voltage | **0**: non-electrical work | **T**: live working |
| **H**: high voltage | **1**: electrical operative | **V**: work near live parts |
| | **2**: work supervisor | **N**: live cleaning |
| | **C**: lock-out | **X**: special |
| | **R**: servicing and troubleshooting | |
| | **S**: replacement and connection work | |

## Examples of clearances

- **B0, H0, H0V operative**: a person cleared to carry out non-electrical work in an electrical environment.
- **B0, H0, H0V site supervisor**: a person leading a team doing non-electrical work near an electrical hazard (site team leader, maintenance manager…).
- **BS**: simple electrical operations (like-for-like replacement of fuses or devices, connecting equipment to a circuit). For inexperienced staff, BS clearance comes with extra knowledge (wiring basics, reading plans…).
- **BE switching, HE switching**: routine operating actions on low or high-voltage installations (resetting a protective device).
- **BC**: in charge of lock-out.
- **BR**: in charge of interventions; allows, for example, troubleshooting, live connections, tests and measurements.
- **Low voltage**: for example B1, B1V, B2, B2V test, BR, BC, BE test, measurement, verification.
- **High voltage**: for example B1, B2, BR, BC, H1, H2, HC, HE.
- **B1**: an electrician working away from bare live parts, always acting on instructions.
- **B1V**: the same tasks as B1, but near bare live parts.
